About CraveIt

Support that lives between consultations.

CraveIt is a women's health and nutrition app centred on the menstrual cycle, food preferences, cravings, symptoms and everyday wellbeing.

It is designed to help a woman notice patterns rather than prescribe a single definition of what healthy should look like.

We are not trying to replace clinicians. We are building the layer of support that exists between consultations.

“Helping women observe, reflect, learn — and arrive at better questions about their own bodies.”

Why we are building it

Women are given fragments of information about their bodies, nutrition and menstrual health — and far less support in connecting those pieces to their lived, everyday experience.

Our mission is to create a trusted, human-centred space where women can better understand their own patterns and make more informed food and wellbeing choices.

We want food to remain food — while still recognising its role in nutritional wellbeing.

The focus is forward: to uplift and to support.

What CraveIt is

Five capabilities, one experience.

Designed to work together rather than as five separate apps.

  • 01

    Cycle-Aware

    Your cycle recorded alongside mood, cramps, bloating, digestion, sleep and cravings.

  • 02

    Food-Aware

    Cravings and food choices are understood in context — never treated as a behavioural failure.

  • 03

    Guidance-Led

    Relevant nutritional and recipe guidance, shaped by what you choose to share.

  • 04

    Whole-Person

    Nutrition sits alongside menstrual and everyday wellbeing rather than isolated from it.

  • 05

    Clinically Conscious

    Designed to support evidence-informed education and decision-making, while holding a clear boundary between general guidance and medical diagnosis or treatment.

How the experience connects

Notice. Record. Understand. Act.

CraveIt joins three things a woman already knows about herself — and returns what may be relevant.

  1. 1

    Notice

    What she is experiencing — symptoms, mood, digestion, sleep.

  2. 2

    Record

    What she is craving or interested in eating, on her own terms.

  3. 3

    Understand

    What may be relevant to her cycle, stated goals, conditions and dietary preferences.

  4. 4

    Act

    Nutrition and recipe guidance she can use today — and better questions for her clinician.

CraveIt is being shaped together with the health professionals we are bringing on board. Cycle predictions are estimates, and every recommendation is general nutrition support — not a diagnosis, treatment or substitute for care.

Our philosophy

Food should be celebrated, explored and supportive.

What We Move Away From

  • A calorie counter at the centre of the experience.
  • A requirement to continually quantify food.
  • A visual standard for what a woman's body is supposed to look like.
  • Blanket rules applied to every woman's health.

What We Make Room For

  • Curiosity and intuition alongside structured guidance.
  • Understanding her experiences instead of fearing them.
  • Deep customisation across health, cycle and personal goals.
  • Encouragement toward appropriate professional care.

Holistic by design

One experience, not five apps.

The Market Today

Most products are built around one part of the picture — the cycle, or food, or symptoms, or recipes.

A woman has to download several apps to assemble a single picture of her own health.

Recommendations tend to apply a blanket rule to facets of health that are not the same for every woman.

Where CraveIt Sits

One holistic experience, with deeper customisation at the core of the product rather than tucked away in a setting.

Multiple factors — cycle, goals, conditions, dietary preference — are taken into account before anything is recommended.

The opportunity is integration, not another single-purpose tracker.

Boundaries we protect

Support, never instead of care.

The lines we hold ourselves to — written down now, before there is pressure to loosen them, and the lines we are asking clinicians to hold us to.

  • 1

    CraveIt is not intended to diagnose disease or medical conditions.

  • 2

    It does not replace a dietitian, doctor, gynaecologist, psychologist or other qualified professional.

  • 3

    We will not imply that nutrition alone can independently treat complex reproductive or hormonal conditions.

  • 4

    We are working to make content clearly separate established evidence, plausible mechanisms and hypotheses.

  • 5

    Where symptoms may warrant assessment — a very long cycle or prolonged bleeding, for example — the app points her towards a clinician: escalation, not self-treatment.

  • 6

    Personalisation should improve relevance — never create an illusion of clinical certainty.

Please note: CraveIt is not a medical device and does not give medical advice. Cycle predictions are estimates, so don't use CraveIt to prevent a pregnancy or to plan one. Allergy and diet filters are a help, not a guarantee — if you have a food allergy, always check ingredients and product labels yourself. Talk to a healthcare provider about medical concerns or before changing your diet.

For clinicians and dietitians

The gap we are closing.

If CraveIt is going to earn trust in women's health, the information and language inside it need to withstand professional scrutiny.

What We Need to Strengthen

  1. 01Clinical and dietetic vetting of the information and terminology we use.
  2. 02Guidance on where evidence is established, emerging, mixed or insufficient.
  3. 03Review of how cycle symptoms and nutritional concepts are framed, so we never overstate causation.
  4. 04Input into safety boundaries, escalation points and referral language.
  5. 05Perspective on how real women experience these issues in practice.
  6. 06A clear view of where digital tools genuinely help — and where they can cause harm.

What we are asking for

We are looking for more than a sign-off. We want a clinical voice in the room while the product is still being shaped.

That means challenging assumptions, identifying blind spots, refining language, strengthening evidence standards — and helping us decide what should never be included.

A woman should feel more informed and more connected to her own experience after using CraveIt — not more anxious, judged or confused.

How Involvement Could Look

  • Reviewing the language and claims inside the product
  • Setting the evidence standard for what reaches users
  • Defining safety flags and referral prompts
  • Sitting in product decisions as a standing clinical advisor
  • Shaping the future clinical and research components

Questions to Explore Together

  • What would make you trust a digital women's health platform enough to recommend it to a patient?
  • Which parts of the menstrual and nutrition conversation are underserved between consultations?
  • Which common claims about hormones, cravings and nutrition should CraveIt actively avoid?
  • Where should the app draw the line between useful education and clinical advice?
  • What safety flags, referral prompts or review processes would you expect?
  • How do we design for women as they actually live, rather than an idealised patient?
